2026 reading list (so far) // 15-11-26

--------------=================--------------

1_. Underlord - Will Wight // 01-Jan-26 // ★★★★☆

book 6 in Will Wight's 'Unsouled' series, continuing on from where i left off in 2025. as my first book of the year it was a great read, Will Wight is an amazing storyteller and world builder. thoroughly enjoyed how the story and characters progressed and was incredibly keen to continue reading the series (as you'll see).

2_. Uncrowned - Will Wight // 03-Jan-26 // ★★★★☆

book 7 in the 'Unsouled' series, i was so excited to keep reading the series after 'Underlord' that i managed to read this book in 2 days. really incredible progression fantasy that just kept getting better and better. it has definitely become one of my favourite book series of all time.

3_. Dungeon Crawler Carl - Matt Dinniman // 10-Jan-26 // ★★★☆☆

Matt Dinniman's 'Dungeon Crawler Carl' came with many great recommendations from friends, and i was quote excited to read this. i have a bit of a love-hate relationship with this book; on the one hand the premise was super interesting and fun, end of the world apocalypse turned dungeon crawler irl video game (what's not to be excited about). but i couldn't get past the vulgar descriptions of some of the creatures, and the recurrent foot fetish joke? idk man

4_. The Restaurant at the End of the Universe - Douglas Adams // 11-Jan-26 // ★★★☆☆

The Restaurant at the End of the Universe was a cool continuation of the 'Hitchhikers Guide to the Galaxy' saga, it took a little while to get through it as i lost a bit of interest about halfway through but ended up being a cool story nonetheless. definitely doesn't live up to book 1 but was still an enjoyable read.

5_. Born to Exile - Phyllis Eisenstein // 08-Feb-26 // ★★☆☆☆

an odd book that i picked up from an op shop based on looks alone. the initial premise was promising, orphan boy who possessed a bloodline power giving him the ability to teleport wherever he thinks of (kinda cool). the book ended up being quite lackluster and weird? very explicit incest overtones and a generally dull resolution. strange.

6_. Rhythm of War - Brandon Sanderson // 04-Mar-26 // ★★★★★

book 4 of Brandon Sanderson's 'The Stormlight Archive' and HO man was it incredible. it is difficult to put into words just how good Brandon Sanderson is at world building, story telling and character development. i could end up writing an entire book about how i feel about The Stormlight Archive, absolutely incredible. easily one of the best reads of the year.

7_. Brave New World - Aldous Huxley // 13-May-26 // ★★★☆☆

i read Brave New World with friends as a part of a work book club that existed for all of 1 book. the core concepts of the book were very interesting and i felt they were still relevant to current_year. i did however lose interest towards the end of the book and found it a struggle to finish. not horrible overall but not my favourite.

8_. Wintersteel - Will Wight // 13-May-26 // ★★★★★

yet another banger from Will Wight continuing the 'Cradle' series, incredible world building, character development and story progression. couldn't get enough of Lindon and Yerin's advancement and growth within the world. it was super enjoyable to begin to learn more about the greater powers at play. definitely solidified my previous statement that 'Cradle' has become one of my favourite book series.

9_. Gogmagog - Jeff Noon, Steve Beard // 14-May-26 // ★★★☆☆

a very interesting read, recommended by a fantasy book thread online while looking for similar styles of writing to Will Wight. i really loved the setting of 'Gogmagog'; post-war, steampunk fantasy world built around The River Nysis (a giant river posessed by the decaying ghost of a dragon). however, the characters realy lost me. vulgar, messy, and lackluster; it was difficult to sympathise, let alone really care about one character.

10_. Ludluda - Jeff Noon, Steve Beard // 03-Aug-26 // ★★★☆☆

given the way i spoke about 'Gogmagog' you wouldn't think i'd bother reading the second book, but the world itself was so interesting and different i was intrigued enough to finish off the duology. 'Ludluda' picks up where the first book left off and essentially could have just been one bigger book instead of a 2 book series honeslty. same criticism as the first but it did have quite a satisfying ending.

11_. The Eye of the World - Robert Jordan // 03-Aug-26 // ★★★★★

before reading 'Eye of the World' i was convinced that no other fantasy series would be able to dethrone 'The Stormlight Archive' as arguably one of the best fantasy series of all time. was i wrong. its clear to see Robert Jordans literary influence on Sanderson but the way he weaves a story is hard to parallel. rich, diverse, and interesting characters; it was an incredibly enjoyable experience to follow the group from their small village town to the world beyond.

12_. The Great Hunt - Robert Jordan // 19-Aug-26 // ★★★★★

you would think it hard to back up such a highly regarded first book byt Jordan does it again. the depth that he puts into each character in this book is incredible and honestly refreshing that it's not another fantasy book filled with sunshine and rainbows. each character experiences turmoil of their own and struggle to come to terms with who they really are and what they're being forced to become by 'The Pattern". loved it.
